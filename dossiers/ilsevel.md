## Appearance

Tall and willowy, silver-white hair cut bluntly at the jaw because long hair "gets in the ink". Pale grey eyes behind round brass-framed spectacles she does not need (they were her mentor's). Wears the long blue robes of a Lamplighter archivist with ink stains on the cuffs and a satchel of books that is always too heavy. Her staff is a Lamplighter's lantern-pole with the lantern removed and a floating blue crystal in its place.

**Sprite notes:** tallest party sprite; satchel on right hip; Pell perches on her shoulder in overworld; idle animation is pushing up her spectacles.

## Backstory

Ilsevel was born in **Hollowmere** in the Thornwild, the youngest of five in a family of elven tree-singers who expected her to sing trees too. She preferred the travelling Lamplighters who came through once a year with their books and their stories of roads. At 92 she walked out of the forest and became a **Wick** (apprentice) at Lamplighters' Hall in Highcrest — the first elf in a century.

Her teacher was the High Lamplighter herself, **Ysolde Marr**, who saw in her the same hunger she had once had. For seventeen years Ysolde taught her lantern-craft, history and evocation; Ilsevel loved her as the parent she had chosen. In 1036 AK Ysolde went into the Underdeep on "an inspection" and never came back. The Order declared her lost. Ilsevel never believed it.

She has spent eleven years as an archivist, quietly collecting every report of failing lanterns, convinced the pattern will lead to her mentor. When the Order needed someone to inspect "minor failures" on the Lantern Road — a job no senior Lamplighter wanted — she volunteered.

## Personality

- **Traits:** Corrects people (gently, constantly). Takes notes during conversations, battles and once, memorably, a funeral. Hums when she reads.
- **Ideal:** *Knowledge.* "The truth is the only light that doesn't need tending."
- **Bond:** Ysolde Marr — the woman she would follow anywhere, which will become the problem.
- **Flaw:** Trusts written records over people's words; assumes she is the smartest person in the room and is usually right, which makes it worse when she isn't.
- **Fears:** being ordinary; being abandoned again.
- **Loves:** first editions, star charts, moon-pears, Pell, a well-made argument.
- **Dislikes:** being touched without warning, people who don't return books, anyone who says "it's just magic".

## Voice sheet

- **Rhythm:** long, precise sentences with subordinate clauses, then a sudden short honest one. Few contractions. Says "technically". Cites books with titles and dates.
- **Sample lines:**
  1. "Technically, I am not lost. I simply have not yet found where I am."
  2. "According to Halbrecht's *Lantern Theory* — the 1002 edition, not the abridged one — this core should still be warm."
  3. "Pell says you are an idiot. I am paraphrasing. He was ruder."
  4. "Fire. Yes. I can do fire."
  5. "That is not how the Lattice works. That is not how *anything* works."
  6. "I wrote it down. I always write it down."
  7. "She taught me to read the dark. I did not think she meant this literally."
  8. "Please stop helping."
  9. "I am a hundred and twenty-three years old. I am allowed to be frightened."
  10. "One more mile." *(Ysolde's phrase; the audience should learn to dread hearing it from her.)*

- She has been writing letters to Ysolde for eleven years and keeping them in her satchel, unsent. There are 132. (Discovered by Tamsin in C3.)
- She once tried to read a sealed book in the Hall's restricted stacks and was punished; she memorised the first page: a diagram of a ninth star in the Lantern constellation. (Pay-off: C6.)

## Growth arc

- **Tier 1:** a scholar who has never been in danger; learns she can be brave.
- **Tier 2:** learns her mentor is alive and leading the Unlit; must choose between Ysolde and the party (C4).
- **Tier 3:** reads the truth in the Archive Beneath and half-agrees with Ysolde; the party's argument becomes her argument.
- **Tier 4:** becomes the Lamplighter Ysolde should have been: the one who shares the truth instead of acting alone.
