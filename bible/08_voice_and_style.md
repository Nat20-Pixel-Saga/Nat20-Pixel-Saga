# Voice and Writing Style

## 1. The narrator

- **Who:** an unnamed storyteller, implied to be someone telling this tale years later.

> SECRET (reveal: C10, final episode): the storyteller is Wil Fenn, grown old. Never hint at this before C10.

- **Person and tense:** third person, present tense in scenes ("Brannoc raises his shield."), past tense in recaps.
- **Register:** plain, warm, specific. Short sentences. One vivid detail per beat, not three.
- **Length per text box:** ≤ 140 characters. The narrator window shows four lines (about 170 characters); a box with a portrait shows three (about 110). A longer line is not cut: it goes on to a second page of the same box, turning at a sentence end, so a final short sentence lands on its own (a punchline can use this). Split longer thoughts into two boxes.
- **Dice lines:** the narrator never states the number — the overlay does. The narrator describes the result: "The blade finds the gap in the goblin's shield."
- **Humour:** dry and observational. The narrator may be gently amused by the party; never mocks them.

### Narrator examples

> The lantern gutters once. Then the road is dark.

> Tamsin's hand is in the merchant's pocket before he finishes saying "honest".

> The fireball blooms. When the smoke clears, three goblins have decided to be somewhere else.

## 2. Dialogue rules

1. **Max 2 text boxes per speaker in a row.** Break speeches with action or another voice.
2. **No exposition dumps.** If a character explains lore, another character reacts within two boxes.
3. **No modern slang or idioms** ("okay", "cool", "no way", "literally", "awesome", "guys"). Use the in-world swears in `01_world_and_cosmology.md` §8.
4. **Names over pronouns** at the start of every scene; the viewer may have missed yesterday's episode.
5. **Callbacks are good.** Reuse a phrase from an earlier episode at least once a week (look it up on the wiki's quotes page).
6. **Contractions:** Tamsin and Brannoc use them freely; Ilsevel rarely; Oriel normally.
7. Each episode ends with a line from a party member, not the narrator, except finales.

## 3. Banned clichés and patterns

- "Little did they know…"
- "A chill ran down their spine."
- "It's quiet. Too quiet."
- "Let's do this!"
- Characters announcing their feelings in full sentences ("I am sad because…"). Show it.
- "Suddenly" more than once per episode.
- Ending a scene with "And so, the adventure began."
- Describing every hit as "devastating" or every enemy as "menacing".
- Fourth-wall jokes about dice, games or rules. The characters do not know they are in a game.

## 5. Episode text structure

```
COLD OPEN (≤ 3 boxes): "Previously…" recap in past tense.
TITLE CARD: "Episode N — <Title>"
SCENES: 3–6 scenes, each starting with a location card ("Brindle Cross — The Crooked Kettle — Night").
DICE: every roll as an overlay cue, then a narrator or character line reacting.
END: party line, then "Next time: <teaser ≤ 60 characters>".
```

## 6. Titles

- Episode titles: 1–4 words, evocative, often a quoted line or place. ("Lantern Thirty-Seven", "Wolves at the Birches", "The Price of a Lie").
- Never use a title that spoils that episode's twist.

## 7. Naming conventions for new NPCs and places

- **Corvane humans:** Anglo-Norman flavour — Edda, Tobin, Maddy, Wil, Rook, Corin, Aldous, Petra.
- **Dwarves:** Germanic-Norse compounds, clan names are oaths or tools — Ironvow, Stoneback, Aldhammer; given names Brannoc, Hilde, Gerda, Dorrek.
- **Elves:** flowing, -el/-wyn/-ith endings — Ilsevel, Ellarin, Sorrelwyn.
- **Halflings:** cosy surnames from plants and homes — Underbough, Thistlewhit, Mossfoot.
- **Goblins:** harsh one- or two-syllable — Skarrow, Rusk, Nib, Gorrit.
- **Saffron Empire:** Persian–Arabic sounds without real words — Iskarah, Zahrun, Ombrasel.
- Avoid names of characters from any existing fiction or game. When in doubt, generate three and pick the least familiar.
