# World and Cosmology

## 1. Talmerra at a glance

Talmerra is a temperate-to-tropical world with one large continent, **the Vael**, ringed by island chains. Most people live inside the light of the lantern network — along roads, rivers and coasts. Beyond it lie wilds that are dangerous by day and lethal by night.

- **Population:** humans are most numerous; dwarves dominate the mountains; elves the old forests; halflings live everywhere, often unnoticed; gnomes, orcs, dragonborn, goliaths and tieflings (SRD species) appear as NPCs.
- **Technology:** late-medieval. Printing presses exist in Highcrest. Clockwork is a gnomish curiosity. Gunpowder does not exist.
- **Literacy:** common in cities, rare in villages. Lamplighters teach reading to anyone who asks.
- **Currency:** the crown (gp), the shilling (sp), the penny (cp). Ten pennies to a shilling, ten shillings to a crown. A platinum **sovereign** (pp) is worth ten crowns.
- **Languages:** Common (the Vael trade tongue), Dwarvish, Elvish, Halfling, Goblin, Giant, Draconic, Sylvan, Primordial dialects, Infernal, Abyssal, Deep Speech, Thieves' Cant.

## 2. The lantern network

- **What a lantern is.** A black-iron post, three to six metres tall, topped with a glass housing. Inside floats a fist-sized **lantern-core**: a faceted crystal that holds a mote of divine flame. A healthy core burns golden-white and needs re-kindling by a Lamplighter once a year at a temple altar.
- **What it does.** A lantern's light makes a **ward** (radius 60–300 metres, depending on size). Inside a ward, the Unlight cannot enter, and undead and fiends are uneasy (they can enter but feel it).
- **The roads.** Lanterns line the great roads at intervals of roughly one per mile, so travellers can walk from ward to ward. Towns have dozens. Capitals have hundreds, plus a great **Beacon** in the central temple.
- **Who pays.** Each lantern is maintained by a local tithe of prayer: a short dawn or dusk devotion at the lantern. In practice, people pray to whichever of the Eight they prefer; the flame accepts all of it.
- **What a dead lantern looks like.** The glass greys, the core turns cloudy and cold to the touch, and within days the Unlight seeps in at night: colours drain from the grass, sounds go flat, frost forms in summer.

## 3. The Unlight

The Unlight is not darkness. A torch still shows you the path; the Unlight simply makes the world *less*. In its fringes colours fade to grey. In its depths, memories slip, warmth leaves the body and people stop being able to name things they love.

- **Fringe Unlight** (dead lantern, first nights): eerie but harmless; animals flee.
- **Deep Unlight** (wilds, long-dead roads, Underdeep): creatures who end a long rest in it gain exhaustion; see `10_rules_of_play.md` §6.
- **Creatures of the Unlight:** the **hollowed** (people and animals who stayed too long; use SRD undead and shadow stat blocks re-skinned as colourless, silent figures), and **gloams** (shapeless predators; use SRD shadow, wraith and later specters).

> SECRET (reveal: C6, Underdeep Descent, beat "The Bleaching Hall"): The Unlight is the slow death of the First Light — the ninth power the Eight devoured at the dawn of the world. Its corpse became the Sundered Planes; its fading warmth leaves Talmerra a little colder every century. The lanterns don't fight the Unlight; they hide it, using flame the gods take from mortal prayer and from the First Light's remaining embers.

## 4. Magic

### 4.1 Arcane magic

Arcane magic is the study of the world's underlying patterns — **the Weave of Talmerra**, which scholars call *the Lattice*. Wizards learn it from books; sorcerers have it in the blood; warlocks borrow it from patrons; bards hear it as music.

- Spellbooks are prized. A wizard's spellbook is shown on screen as a glowing open book; copying a spell costs gold and time, as in the SRD.
- Arcane magic works inside the Unlight, but its colours drain: a Fire Bolt cast in deep Unlight is grey-white. This is a purely visual rule and a useful storytelling tell.

### 4.2 Divine magic

Divine magic is divine flame, channelled by clerics and paladins through faith. A cleric's spells visually include a golden spark at the holy symbol.

- **The Dimming affects divine magic narratively, not mechanically**, until Campaign 8. From Campaign 8, beats may impose specific effects (e.g. a temple whose altar is dead cannot be used for the *Hallow* spell). Never reduce a cleric's spell slots by fiat.

### 4.3 Primal magic

Druids and rangers draw from the living world. Primal magic does not depend on the lanterns, which makes druids both respected and suspected; some Unlit agents are disillusioned druids.

### 4.4 Magic in society

- Most villages have a hedge-witch or a priest who knows a cantrip or two.
- Healing magic beyond *Cure Wounds* is expensive (temples charge for *Lesser Restoration*, *Raise Dead*, etc., at SRD-consistent prices).
- Spellcasting in a city without a licence is frowned on in Highcrest and illegal in the Saffron Empire.

## 7. Moons and sky

- **Seln**, the silver moon, 28-day cycle. Werecreatures follow it.
- **Ember**, the small red moon, 91-day cycle. When Ember is full, divine flame burns brighter (narrative only).
- **The Lantern Stars:** a constellation of eight stars, one per god. During the series, one star dims each campaign from C3 onwards — an on-screen clock for the audience.

## 8. Everyday life details (for flavour)

- **Greeting:** "Light on your road." Reply: "And on yours."
- **Swearing:** "Ash and cinders!", "By the Anvil!", "Eight blind!" (mildly blasphemous), "Unlit take it."
- **Food:** brown bread, river trout, mutton stew, honey cakes, dwarven stone-baked flatbread, elven moon-pears, halfling seed-cakes, Ember Coast pepper fish.
- **Drink:** small beer, cider, Aldhammer black stout, Gloaming dew-wine (dangerous), Saffron Empire spiced coffee.
- **Inns** keep a small lantern in the window; a dark window means "no room" — or that something is wrong.
